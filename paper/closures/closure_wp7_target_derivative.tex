\begin{proof}
\emph{Target bound \eqref{eq:target-num}.} By the triangle inequality,
\[
 \norm{T^{K,\delta,M}-T}_\infty
 \le\norm{T^{K,\delta,M}-T^{K,\delta}}_\infty
 +\norm{T^{K,\delta}-T^{K}}_\infty
 +\norm{T^{K}-T}_\infty,
\]
and the three terms are bounded by $C\eta_M$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:operator},
uniform on the compact classes; $C\eta_M^2$ after the first-order
correction \eqref{eq:op-corrected}), $C\delta^\kappa$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:temporal-projection} or Proposition~\ref{prop:left}), and
$CK^{-\rho_F}$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:law-rate}), respectively; all constants are
uniform over the parameter rectangle because the underlying mean and
variance tails are.

\emph{Derivative bound \eqref{eq:derivative-num}.} It suffices to prove that
the intervention derivatives of the \emph{primitives} obey tail bounds with
the same spectral exponents; the chain rule through the smooth Gaussian
formulas then transfers them to $DT$ exactly as in the zeroth-order proofs.
For the mean: $m_a(w)=\sum_ku_{0,k}\int_0^Tw_k(t)e^{-a\lambda_kt}\,dt$, so
\[
 \partial_a^{\,j}m_a(w)
 =\sum_k(-1)^ju_{0,k}\lambda_k^{\,j}\int_0^Tt^{\,j}w_k(t)
 e^{-a\lambda_kt}\,dt,
 \qquad j=1,2,
\]
and by Cauchy--Schwarz with
$\norm{t^je^{-a\lambda_kt}}_{L^2(0,T)}^2
=\int_0^Tt^{2j}e^{-2a\lambda_kt}dt
\le\Gamma(2j+1)(2\underline a\lambda_k)^{-(2j+1)}$,
\[
 \lambda_k^{\,j}\norm{t^je^{-a\lambda_kt}}_{L^2}
 \le C_j\,\lambda_k^{\,j}\lambda_k^{-(2j+1)/2}
 =C_j\,\lambda_k^{-1/2},
\]
the same spectral order as the underlying kernel
$\norm{e^{-a\lambda_kt}}_{L^2}\le C\lambda_k^{-1/2}$. Hence the tail sums
defining the mean error repeat verbatim with unchanged exponent
$(r_0+1+2s)/2$, through second order in $a$.
For the variance: with the transfer kernel
$g_{a,k,w}(s)=\int_s^Tw_k(t)e^{-a\lambda_k(t-s)}dt=(\mathcal K_{a,k}\ast
w_k)(s)$ a Volterra convolution against $\mathcal K(u)=e^{-a\lambda_ku}
\mathbf 1_{u\ge0}$,
\[
 \partial_a^{\,j}g_{a,k,w}
 =(-\lambda_k)^j\big(u^{\,j}\mathcal K\big)\ast w_k,
 \qquad
 \norm{\lambda_k^{\,j}u^{\,j}\mathcal K}_{L^1(0,\infty)}
 =\lambda_k^{\,j}\,\frac{\Gamma(j+1)}{(a\lambda_k)^{j+1}}
 \le C_j\lambda_k^{-1},
\]
which is the same $L^1$ order as
$\norm{\mathcal K}_{L^1}=1/(a\lambda_k)$. Young's inequality
($L^1\ast L^2\to L^2$) then reproduces the variance-tail exponent
$\beta+2+2s$ for $\partial_a^{\,j}V_a(w)$, $j=1,2$. Temporal and
bounded-operator errors for the derivative surfaces follow by applying the
same projection and perturbation arguments to these differentiated kernels,
which lie in the same kernel classes; and the derivative of the Gaussian CDF
composition contributes bounded factors by the uniform non-degeneracy
$\inf_{a,w}V_a(w)>0$. Combining gives \eqref{eq:derivative-num}.
\end{proof}
